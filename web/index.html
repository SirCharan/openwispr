Features

Twelve things, done right.

Side ADictation
  1. 01On-device Whisper

    Speech is transcribed on your Mac through WhisperKit. No account. After the one-time model download it works offline.

  2. 02Live preview

    A floating pill shows your words while you speak. Release the key and the text pastes at the cursor.

  3. 03Tidy by default

    Fillers removed and sentences capitalised with no AI model. An AI cleanup and a rewrite style are there if you want them.

  4. 04Learns your words

    A custom dictionary for product names, teammates and jargon. Add a term once and it is typed right.

  5. 05Snippets

    Say "add my email" and your address pastes itself. Set a phrase once for your links and your sign-off.

  6. 0613 languages

    Auto-detected or pinned. Speak Hindi and get Roman script, or have your speech translated to English.

Side BAround it
  1. 07History and Insights

    Every dictation saved and searchable. Insights shows your pace, words per day and where you dictate.

  2. 08Quiet where you want it

    Turn dictation off per app, switch sound cues on or off, and pick the light or dark theme.

  3. 09Hands-free and recovery

    Double-tap the trigger to dictate until the next tap. The pill warns "Mic is silent". A failed dictation is kept so you can retry it.

  4. 10Agent skill and CLI

    An openwispr command with JSON output, and a skill that lets a coding agent read your history. See Developers.

  5. 11Updates in the app

    New versions arrive inside the app through Sparkle. Check from the menu, or let it check by itself.

  6. 12Ready at login

    Launch at login is on by default, and the speech model loads faster after its first run.

Meetings

The minutes write themselves.

OpenWispr records your mic and the call's audio on your Mac, labels who said what, and saves the transcript as Markdown in ~/Documents/OpenWispr. No bot joins the call.

  • Offers to recordIt spots a Zoom, Google Meet or Teams call and asks "Start recording?". You can say yes once, or always for that app.
  • Real speaker namesZoom names are read through Accessibility. Google Meet names come from a small Chrome extension that ships with the app and is loaded unpacked. Other calls get Speaker A, B and so on.
  • Per-meeting statsTalk share, pace, turns and longest monologue for each speaker, plus questions and interruptions for the call.
  • SummariesA summary uses a local Ollama model or your own OpenAI or OpenRouter key. Macs with Apple Intelligence on macOS 26 summarise on-device with no setup.
  • Stops by itselfOn Google Meet, recording stops about 10 seconds after you leave. Any call stops after 10 minutes of silence.
  • Warns on a silent callIf you are talking and nothing arrives from the other side, a banner says "No audio from the call is being captured".
Developers

Your transcripts, on the command line.

The app ships an openwispr command.

  • JSON by default. Every command prints one JSON envelope, so scripts and agents can read it.
  • Agent skill. openwispr skill install copies a skill into ~/.claude/skills, so a coding agent can read your dictation and meeting history through the CLI.
  • Links that ask first. openwispr:// links start and stop dictation and meetings. A start link from outside the CLI shows a confirmation with Cancel and Allow.
# the command lives inside the app bundle
$ cd /Applications/OpenWispr.app/Contents/Helpers

$ ./openwispr history search "launch"
$ ./openwispr meetings list
$ ./openwispr transcribe memo.m4a
$ ./openwispr skill install

# the same actions as links
$ open "openwispr://dictation/start"
$ open "openwispr://meeting/stop"

Does any audio or text leave my Mac?›
Your audio, transcripts and history stay on the Mac. There is no account and no analytics in the app. What does use the network: the one-time speech model download, the update check and the licence check, which sends your key and the Mac's name to the payment provider. Text goes to an AI provider only if you add your own API key. The privacy page lists all of it.
Is the app signed?›
It is not notarized yet. macOS blocks the first launch, and you confirm once under System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway. Signed and notarized builds come with the Developer ID release.
Does it work offline?›
Yes. After the one-time model download of about 1.5 GB, dictation and meeting transcripts need no network. The licence is re-checked every 7 days, with a 21-day grace period if you are offline.
Which Macs does it run on?›
Apple Silicon Macs on macOS 14 or later. Intel Macs are not supported.
Is it really open source? Then why pay?›
Yes. The source is MIT on GitHub, and a build you make from source needs no key. What you pay for is the ready-made build, its updates and the download that comes with your key.
How is it different from Wispr Flow?›
OpenWispr processes your speech on your Mac and costs $15 once. Wispr Flow processes in the cloud and costs $15 per user per month billed monthly, or $12 billed annually, with a free tier that has a weekly word limit.
